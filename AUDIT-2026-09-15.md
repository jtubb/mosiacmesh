## 0.1 Where the problem actually stands

The clock layer is **finished**. Per your v1–v4 diagnostic ladder: GoTime tracks ground truth to ~4 ms (optimal), crystals are healthy and common-mode (−19 ppm, 9 ppm spread), and the real cross-screen clock disagreement is **~27 ms median / 38 ms p90** (fit-based, trimmed). AP tuning moved nothing. The synctool `clock-disagreement` number (~300–500 ms) is outlier-and-uplink-jitter-dominated and **unreliable**.

So the remaining visible desync is the **video layer: ~150–220 ms video-offset**, roughly **7× the clock floor**. That is where the headroom is, exactly as your 2026-08-03 reframe concluded.

Within the video layer the record establishes:
- Rate **actuator** works ~1:1 across 0.5→2.0 via CMTimebase (open-loop, 5-load aggregate). **Not** the problem.
- Spurious 30 s seeks **fixed** by Lever A (30 s → 59 s median interval).
- Loop-boundary stall **fixed** (explicit-loop restart, merged).
- Cold-load / verr=3 **still open** (re-`load()` is the wrong recovery; recreate-element untested).
- Steady-state `ferr` sits at **−77…−206 ms, all behind clock, 128 ms cross-screen spread.**

## 0.2 The open question: is that −125 ms droop, or bias?

**Two mechanisms predict the observed `ferr` almost identically, and the record does not distinguish them.**

**(a) Classic P-droop against a real decode deficit.** A P-only law needs a standing error to hold a correction: `ferr = deficit / GAIN`. With the measured ~2.5 % deficit and `RATE_GAIN = 0.0002` (`drift-steer.js:11`):
> 0.025 / 0.0002 = **125 ms**

**(b) Sensor staleness bias.** `v.currentTime` is a **250 ms zero-order-hold with no timestamp** — verified at source this pass:

| Step | Code |
|---|---|
| Observer at 250 ms, **main queue** | `tweak/mmvideo/MMTransplantEngine.m:160` — `addPeriodicTimeObserverForInterval:CMTimeMake(1, 4)` |
| Value cached, not timestamped | `MMTransplantEngine.m:164` — `eng->curTime = mm_secs(t);` |
| Published to a global | `MMTransplantEngine.m:172` — `g_curTime = eng->curTime;` |
| JS getter returns the cache | `MMTransplantEngine.m:287` (`mm_ctrlCT`) and `:235` (`mm_engine_current_time`) |

Consumed raw at `index.html:1084` (`errorMs = v.currentTime*1000 - pos.offsetMs`), sampled every 500 ms (`index.html:1158`). Mean staleness of a 250 ms ZOH is **125 ms**, and it reads as a persistent *negative* `errorMs` — "always behind," which is exactly the observed one-sided signature.

> **Both mechanisms independently predict ≈125 ms, one-signed, behind.** The measured −77…−206 ms is consistent with either, or with a mix.

This matters because **they need opposite fixes.** Droop is cured by an integral. Bias is *immune* to an integral — an integral drives the *measured* error to zero, which parks the *true* position at exactly the staleness. If the residual is mostly (b), any amount of PI/feedforward tuning will keep failing, which is a fair description of the last several arcs.

### Two things the record shows have *not* been tried

1. **PI has never been retried since Lever A.** The recorded reason PI failed was *"an integral can't converge when a spurious seek resets the loop every 30 s."* Lever A removed those seeks. **The precondition that broke PI is gone, and PI was deferred rather than re-tested.** Branch `fix/drift-steer-pi-integral` already exists.
2. **The observer interval was shelved for the wrong reason.** `feat/mmvideo-currenttime-observer` (66 ms / off-main) was built and validated, then marked *"UNNECESSARY — the bimodal does not reproduce at settled full load."* That judged it against **readback noise**. The **staleness bias is not noise** — it is present by construction at every load, and scales directly with the interval. It was never evaluated against the metric that matters.

## 0.3 The discriminating experiment (zero deploy, zero fleet risk, one screen)

The two hypotheses split cleanly on one measurement, and **`ratetest.html` + `js/ratetestflag.js` already give you an in-webclip probe harness** (it runs inside Web.app where mmvideo lives — `uiopen`/Safari is not representative).

**Probe:** sample `v.currentTime` at ~20 Hz alongside `GoTime.now()`. Then compute two numbers:

- **`ferr_loop`** — what the drift controller currently sees (the 500 ms-sampled raw value). Expect ≈ −125 ms.
- **`ferr_true`** — the same error computed **only at staircase step edges**, where staleness ≈ 0. (Equivalently: the running **max of `ct_i·1000 − t_i`**, which is attained by the freshest sample.)

**Read the result:**

| Outcome | Interpretation | Next move |
|---|---|---|
| `ferr_true ≈ 0`, `ferr_loop ≈ −125` | **Bias dominates.** The video is physically near-correct; the controller is chasing a phantom. | Fix the sensor (§0.4). Do **not** retry PI first. |
| `ferr_true ≈ ferr_loop ≈ −125` | **Droop dominates.** The video really is behind. | Retry PI now that Lever A cleared the seek resets. |
| In between | Both present, in measurable proportion. | Sensor first (it's the cheaper, un-tried one), then PI. |

**Confirmations this same run gives you for free:** that `currentTime` advances in discrete ~250 ms steps (kills or confirms the ZOH premise outright), and the per-screen staleness phase.

**Why this ordering:** it is a *measurement*, on *one screen*, using *existing tooling*, that costs nothing and forecloses a wrong build. That is precisely the discipline the recvTs arc and the cold-load-watchdog arc were missing — both were sound builds against unmeasured premises, and both cost a full cycle.

## 0.4 If bias is implicated — the fix, in risk order

1. **Pure-JS staleness correction (no dylib, no fleet deploy).** Replace the raw read at `index.html:1084` with the max-of-recent-`(ct_i − t_i)` estimator. Cuts residual from *up to 250 ms, per-screen, slowly wandering* to *≈ the polling interval*. Testable on one screen via reload.
2. **Timestamp + extrapolate in the tweak (definitive).** Store `mach_absolute_time()` in the observer block; have `mm_ctrlCT` return `g_curTime + clamp(now − g_curTimeHost) × rate`. Staleness → getter latency, for every consumer, and **robust to main-queue starvation in a way that merely raising the observer rate is not**. Plain C, `mach_absolute_time()` only — no new ObjC class, so it respects the §14 loader fragility that killed KVO. Guard on `e->playing`; clamp (~400 ms) so a starved observer can't run away.
3. **Deploying the existing 66 ms branch** is a weaker version of #2 — it shrinks the bias ~4× but leaves it load-sensitive. Useful mainly as a fast corroboration if the probe is ambiguous.

# PART 1 — Verified defects

## 1.1 Offline detection is dead code — **[VERIFIED]**
**`server.py:2134-2141` · High · Bug**

```python
current_time = time.time()                       # 2120
for client_key, client in settings.clients.items():
    if client.isOnline:
        client.lastSeen = current_time           # 2136 ← refreshes ALL online clients
for client_key, client in settings.clients.items():
    if (current_time - client.lastSeen) > 60 and client.isOnline:   # 2141 ← always 0.0
```

Every client the stale check could apply to had `lastSeen` set to `current_time` moments earlier, so the delta is **exactly 0.0**. The 60 s check can never fire; the silence path to `CLIENTS_WENT_OFFLINE` is unreachable. A client only goes offline on a clean TCP FIN.

**Why it matters:** an iPad that drops WiFi without a FIN — the documented PSM/doze case — stays `isOnline = True` indefinitely. This is very likely the mechanism behind *"discovery API `online` field lies"* (recorded in `coordinated-start-arm-cap`), and plausibly behind the stuck-GO incident that `ARM_HARD_GRACE_MS` was added to paper over.

## 1.2 Reconnect-storm broadcast amplification — **[VERIFIED]**
**`mosaicmesh/broadcast.py:36-59` · High · Performance**

## 1.13 mmcache buffers the WHOLE segment in RAM — caps usable clip length — **[VERIFIED 2026-09-17]**
**`tweak/mmcache/Tweak.x:110` · High (correctness) / blocks a product capability** — the download uses
`NSURLConnection sendSynchronousRequest:returningResponse:error:`, which materializes the **entire
response body as one in-memory `NSData`** before writing it to disk. That is deliberate: reading
`expectedContentLength` off the response is how the truncation guard verifies completeness before
caching, which is what fixed the old verr=3 "cached a truncated .mp4" bug.

The cost is a hard ceiling on segment size. The iPad-1 has `hw.memsize = 258,981,888` (**247 MB
total RAM**, shared with SpringBoard/mediaserverd/lighttpd), Web.app idles at ~37-42 MB RSS, and
[[gameoflife-warmup-jetsam]] records that 51-63 MB RSS was already enough to close webclips out. Every
segment ever shipped has been 3.5-6 MB, so this has never been hit — but a 10-minute PullTest4 render
produced **150 MB per screen**, an allocation ~60% of all physical RAM. It would either fail to
allocate (mmcache acks failure → falls back to streaming 150 MB centrally → AP saturation, see
[[full-video-wifi-bound]]) or trip jetsam and kill the webclip. Nothing relaunches a dead webclip, so
that is a permanently dark screen.

Measured encode rate for sizing: **0.2516 MB/s** of clip at a 0.25 s keyframe grid, **0.128 MB/s** at
1 s. Keeping segments ≤ ~15-20 MB means ≤ ~2 min of clip at a 1 s grid.

**Fix (deferred, recorded 2026-09-17 at the user's direction):** stream to disk instead of buffering —
replace the synchronous call with an async `NSURLConnection` delegate that appends each
`connection:didReceiveData:` chunk to an `NSFileHandle`, and verify the accumulated byte count against
`expectedContentLength` in `connectionDidFinishLoading:` (keeping the guard, losing the buffer). Memory
then stays constant regardless of clip length and the 2-minute cap disappears. It is a second native
tweak to build/canary/deploy, so it was held back while the mmvideo C1 canary is soaking. Workaround in
the meantime: keep clips short enough to stay inside the envelope.

## 1.14 A video's item window silently falls back to 20s unless `/api/media` was called — **[VERIFIED ON-WALL 2026-09-17]**
**`mosaicmesh/render.py:1349` (`_probed_video_seconds`) → `:1373` (`_duration_ms`) · High (correctness)**
— an "Auto"-duration video item gets its window from `_probed_video_seconds`, which is a **read-only
lookup** into `server._video_duration_cache`. That cache is populated by exactly one thing:
`GET /api/media`. On a miss the helper returns `None` and `_duration_ms` falls back to
`DEFAULT_ITEM_DURATION_S * 1000` = **20 000 ms**, silently.

So a video that is added to disk and put on the wall *without anyone opening the admin media list*
plays inside a 20-second window regardless of its real length. Nothing warns: the RENDER path is
unaffected (ffmpeg probes the source directly and encodes the full clip), so the segment on disk is
correct and only the **playback scheduler** is wrong. The two disagree.

**Observed:** `bbb_2min.mp4` (120.000 s) created 21:50, assigned + played 22:01 with no `/api/media`
call in between. On-wall telemetry: `elapsed - offset = 20000` exactly on every screen — the wall
cycled a 120 s clip every 20 s. 12 screens wrapped at the phantom boundary while 4 kept playing to the
file's real end, reading as a ~60 s cross-screen desync that was not a sync fault at all. Diagnosis is
arithmetic on the CLIENTLOG: `offset = ct - err`, then `elapsed - offset` is the window the client is
actually using.

The read-only design is deliberate and correct in itself (the docstring: ffprobe is async/blocking and
`_duration_ms` runs on the wire-build path). The defect is the **silent** fallback to a wrong-by-6x
window with no log line and no distinction between "this is an image/animation, 20 s is right" and
"this is a 10-minute video I failed to measure."

**Fixes, cheapest first:** (a) warm the cache where ffprobe already runs — the render path probes the
source to encode it, so have it populate `_video_duration_cache` as a side effect, which covers every
renderable item by construction; (b) failing that, log a WARNING when a `.mp4/.mov/...` item takes the
default window, so it is visible instead of silent; (c) probe asynchronously on playlist save.
**Workaround used on-wall:** set an explicit `duration` on the item (note this changes `me.duration`,
which is hashed into `render_token`, so it forces a re-render).

## 1.15 (original text, retained for the record) The device cache is effectively COLD after every page reload
**`js/mmCacheBackendMmvideo.js:13,52` + `tweak/mmcache/Tweak.x:184-193` · High (performance/correctness)**
— two facts combine into a re-download storm:

1. `_present` (the token→bytes map behind `mmCache.has()`) is a **plain in-memory JS object that
   starts empty on every page load**. It is populated *only* by `window.__mmCacheDone`, the native
   callback for a fetch that completed **in this page session**. Nothing enumerates the on-disk cache
   at startup. So after any reload the client believes it has cached nothing, however full the
   directory is.
2. The native `fetch` op has **no existing-file fast path** — `handle_mmcache` parses the token and
   goes straight to `dispatch_async_f(..., mm_download)`, which re-downloads the entire segment over
   the network even when the complete file is already sitting at `MM_CACHE_DIR/<token>.mp4`.

Net effect: **every reload re-downloads every segment.** At 3.5-6 MB segments this was cheap and
invisible. At 19 MB it is ~440 MB across 23 screens, throttled 3-at-a-time (`PrecacheWindow`, n=3),
taking 12+ minutes — and during that window screens either stream centrally (which an iPad-1 cannot
sustain for a segment this size) or hang. That is the real engine behind the "latch" cascade seen on
2026-09-17/18, not a video-element bug: reload a screen whose cache is cold and it streams centrally,
stalls, and the poll watchdog escalates to `wd-dead` ("stay black"). Reproduced deliberately on
sign1screen17 (10:14 2026-09-18): RELOAD → `vid:canplay`/`vid:playing` → `rs=4` → ct froze at 116000 →
`wd-retry` → `wd-dead` → back to `rs=0 ct=0`, ~10 s end to end.

There is also a **server/client bookkeeping split**: `client.cachedSegments` (server-side, what
`propagationPercent` reports) survives a reload, while the client's `_present` does not. So the server
can report a screen at 100% while the screen itself believes its cache is empty and streams centrally.
Do not trust `propagationPercent` as evidence of what a screen is actually playing from.

**Fix (small, native, safe):** short-circuit `fetch` when the file already exists — stat
`MM_CACHE_DIR/<token>.mp4` and, if present and non-empty, call `dispatch_done(token, size)` immediately
instead of downloading. This is sound *because* the write path guarantees completeness: `mm_download`
rejects any transfer whose length != `expectedContentLength` **before** writing, and writes with
`writeToFile:atomically:` flag 1, so a partial file can never appear in the directory. Presence
therefore implies validity. That makes `_present` refill from disk at reload speed, makes reloads free,
and removes the window in which screens stream centrally.
Cheap companion: have the JS backend ask the native side to enumerate the directory once at startup,
so `_present` is seeded without even a per-token round trip.

## 1.16 ~~BLOCKER: any mmcache rebuild fails to load~~ — **[RESOLVED 2026-09-18 21:10. ROOT CAUSE: `cp` OVER a live dylib. The binary was never at fault.]**

> **THE FIX: never `cp` over an installed dylib. `rm` it first (or `mv` a temp into place) so the
> new content lands on a FRESH INODE.**
>
> Mutating a dylib in place leaves dyld/Substrate holding a cached signature or page mapping for
> that inode which no longer matches its contents, and the load is rejected. Writing identical
> bytes is harmless (the cache stays consistent), which is exactly why every rollback "worked" and
> made the binary look guilty.
>
> | install method | bytes | result |
> |---|---|---|
> | `cp` OVER existing | new | **0 / 25** across three separate builds |
> | `cp` OVER existing | identical (July rollback) | 5 / 5 first try |
> | **new path** `mmcache2.dylib` | the very bytes that had failed 6× | **1 / 1 first try** |
> | **original path, `rm` + `cp`** | those same bytes | **1 / 1 first try** |
>
> The last two rows are decisive: the same md5 (`eae0e7fb…`) that failed six consecutive times as
> a `cp`-over of `mmcache.dylib` loaded immediately both at a new path and at the original path
> once the old file was removed first. sign1screen12 is now running that build (fast path +
> chunked download) and playing.
>
> **Everything 1.16 originally blamed was wrong.** Not the toolchain, not the symbols, not

**What was ruled out:**
- *Symbols.* v1 added `_stat`, which is genuinely unbindable here and is a real bug (it is absent from
  the `-U` flat-bind list in `tweak/mmcache/Makefile`). Fixed in v2 by taking the filesystem facts via
  NSFileManager/objc_msgSend instead. `llvm-nm -u` diff of v2 and of the baseline rebuild against the
  July binary: **zero** new undefined symbols.
- *Linked dylibs.* Identical list and versions (libobjc, Foundation, CoreFoundation, libSystem,
  CydiaSubstrate).
- *Namespace.* All three are `TWOLEVEL` — not the `dynamic_lookup` failure mode documented in
  mmvideo's Makefile.
- *Mach header.* Identical flags, `ncmds` 17, `sizeofcmds` 1736 on both.
- *Code signature.* Neither old nor new carries `LC_CODE_SIGNATURE`; the old one loads without it.
- *Transport/install.* On-device md5 verified equal to local after every push; install path identical
  to the restore path that works.
- *Not toolchain-wide.* `mmvideo_c1retire.dylib`, built the same day with the same theos, loads and
  has run 14+ h clean. The problem is specific to the mmcache build.

**Still unknown:** the two binaries differ in 1445 bytes, clustered from offset ~0x82f9 in `__LINKEDIT`
with values shifted by a constant +12/+16 — the signature of a **string table that grew by 16 bytes**,
shifting every later offset. Next step is to find which string grew (`llvm-nm -a` local-symbol diff,
or `--macho --link-edit`), since that is the only substantive difference found so far. Caveat on the
controlled-ness of that specific comparison: the baseline was built in `~/mmcache_base` rather than
`~/mmcache`, a 5-character path difference that could itself account for the string growth — but the
two fast-path builds used `~/mmcache` and failed identically, so the conclusion stands.

**RE-TESTED WITH A REBOOT 2026-09-18 16:00-16:38 (operator asked: "reboot or just a respring?").**
The original test used only `killall Web` + relaunch, with a `killall SpringBoard` respring on some
attempts — never a reboot, which the operator's experience says is required to pick up a new tweak.

**functionally identical**: 101 symbols each, same names, same undefined set, same linked dylibs, same
Mach flags/ncmds/sizeofcmds. The only delta is the debug path stab
(`/home/jtubb/mmcache/` vs `/home/jtubb/mmcache_base/`) — 16 bytes that cannot affect dyld.

The conclusion nevertheless survives the reboot test:

| build | pre-reboot (kill + relaunch, 2 resprings) | post-reboot |
|---|---|---|
| July dylib `884ff22c` | 4/4, always first try | **1/1, first try** |
| unmodified rebuild `97d54ca8` | **0/13**, each crashing in dyld | **0/~10**, silent |

Notes that make this trustworthy: every pre-reboot attempt produced its own crash report at the matching

(b) some post-reboot VNC taps landed on Game Center instead of the webclip, which briefly looked like a
coordinate-mapping bug but was transient — the same coordinate worked once the July dylib was in place.

**Still unexplained, and now the sharpest form of the puzzle:** two Mach-O files that are identical in
every inspectable dimension behave differently at load. That points at `__LINKEDIT` content the symbol
table does not show (bind/rebase opcode encoding), or at a linker-version artifact. Note `mmvideo` built
the same day with the same theos DOES load (14+ h on screen5), so the toolchain is not globally broken —
whatever it is, is specific to how mmcache links. The cheapest next experiment is to build mmcache with
mmvideo's known-good flag set (it adds `mmbuiltins.c` and uses `-fobjc-arc -fno-objc-arc-exceptions`,
where mmcache uses `-fno-objc-arc`) and see whether the result loads, before root-causing further.

**Operational rules this produced:**
1. **Symbol gate before every native deploy.** `llvm-nm -u <new> | comm -13 <known-good undefined set>`
   must be empty. `build.sh`'s existing checks (no ObjC classes, no C++ unwind, no unresolved builtins)
   pass on binaries that do not load, so they are necessary but not sufficient.
2. **Keep a known-good dylib on-device** (`/var/mobile/<tweak>_pre_<change>.dylib`) before overwriting.
   That backup is what recovered this screen three times.
3. **Canary means one screen and a rollback you have already tested** — not a fleet push.

## 1.17 PLAY races cache propagation: the src is chosen once, from `cachedSegments` at that instant — **[VERIFIED ON-WALL 2026-09-18]**
**`mosaicmesh/render.py:1526-1533` (`_per_client_items`) · High (correctness)** — the per-client video
src is decided when the PLAY/PREPARE payload is built:

```python
if (prefix == "seg_" and cache_on and seg_key in cached):
    f = "http://127.0.0.1:8080/seg_" + seg_key + ".mp4"   # local, via mmvideo
else:
    f = "/media/" + key + "/" + prefix + token + "_" + str(i) + ext   # central
```

A client whose pull has not completed **at that moment** is handed the CENTRAL url. The cache landing
seconds or minutes later does not change the src the client is already holding, and nothing re-issues
it. On an iPad-1 a 19 MB central stream does not sustain: the element hangs at `ns=2 rs=0`, the poll
watchdog escalates retry → recache → `wd-dead`, and the screen stays black. Nothing relaunches it.

This is the actual cause of the 2026-09-17 "latch" cascade, which I first misattributed to a cache
coldness storm (1.15, retracted). Assign+play fired at 22:08 with propagation at 1/24; propagation
reached 14/24 over the next ~12 min, but the screens that had been handed central urls stayed wedged.
A reload fixed them **instantly** — because the server then rebuilt the payload and, propagation now
being complete, handed them the localhost url.

**Controlled reproduction 2026-09-18 12:39** (fleet restarted by the operator, caches already on disk
from 07:58-08:02, so PLAY did NOT race the pull):

| | 09-17 (PLAY raced the pull) | 09-18 (caches pre-warmed) |
|---|---|---|
| cached screens playing | 7 / 14 | **14 / 14** |
| screens wedged | 7, all WITH caches | 4, all WITHOUT caches |
| cache files rewritten | — | **none** (mtimes untouched) |

`propagationPercent == 100` predicted PLAYING on 14/14. Every remaining stuck screen is one the server
has no `cachedSegments` for; those get the central url and roughly half of them fail to stream it.

**Fixes, in order of value:**
1. **Gate PLAY on propagation** for cache-capable clients — the render is already gated on READY; this
   is the same idea one layer further on. Or hold the un-propagated clients out of the GO set the way
   `ARM_HARD_GRACE_MS` holds un-armable ones, rather than starting them on a url that cannot work.
2. **Re-issue the item payload when a client's cache completes.** `handle_cache_ack` already knows the
   moment `cachedSegments` gains the key; re-sending that client's items there would convert a wedged
   screen into a playing one with no reload.
3. Failing both, have the client re-request its items when `mmCache` reports the token cached.

Note this also means **`propagationPercent` is the single best predictor of whether a screen will
play**, and that starting playback before it reaches 100 across the group is the operational mistake
to avoid.

## 1.18 `mmcache://fetch` is not idempotent — concurrent downloaders share one `.part` — **[FIXED + PROVEN ON-WALL 2026-10-12]**
**`tweak/mmcache/Tweak.x` (`handle_mmcache` fetch path) · High (correctness)** — the handler checked
only for a FINISHED `<token>.mp4` on disk, then unconditionally `dispatch_async_f`'d another
`mm_download` onto the GLOBAL CONCURRENT queue. Nothing tracked work already in progress.

The grants legitimately repeat: `mmCache._pending` is per-page-load, so a webclip reload mid-transfer
loses it while the native download keeps running, and the server re-grants on schedule. Concurrent
instances then share one `.part`, and **every instance that decides `MM_DL_CLEAN` removes and
re-creates that file underneath its siblings** — so bytes could not accumulate and `mm_resume_decide`
never saw a usable partial. Resume was not merely broken, it was *structurally unreachable*.

sign1screen20's log for `seg_4298b787efa0_0` shows the shape exactly: 11 `fetch` lines against 7
terminations, six `fetch` in a row with nothing between them, adjacent failures at 13,631,488 and at
0 (two live writers on one file), and not one `resume … from=` line, ever. The 64 MB segment landed
only once the storm thinned to a single survivor, which then ran all 62 chunks from zero.

**Fix:** `tweak/mmcache/mminflight.h` — a per-token claim taken in the fetch hook and released inside
the main-queue block `dispatch_done`/`dispatch_fail` already post. Main-thread-only, so no lock and
**no new linker symbol** (`llvm-nm -u` identical at 23 undefineds; the 5.1 loader SIGKILLs over one
unbindable symbol). Refuses rather than evicts when full: evicting a live entry would re-permit a
second writer on a `.part` still being written.

**Proof (2026-10-12, sign1screen22, 116,537,316 B):** a forged terminal ack — `handle_cache_ack`
trusts `msg["SRC"]` — released the window while the download ran, and the reconcile re-granted:
```
fetch token=seg_4298b787efa0_0
already downloading token=seg_4298b787efa0_0 (1 in flight) -- dropping duplicate grant
OK token=seg_4298b787efa0_0 bytes=116537316 chunks=112
```
112 is the arithmetic minimum; md5 identical to source. No code change was needed to reproduce the
condition — only the existing protocol.

## 1.19 A cache ack raised while the transport is down is dropped, silently and forever — **[FIXED 2026-09-26]**
**`index.html:2156` · High (correctness)** — the ack was wired as a bare
`if (sock && sock.readyState === SockJS.OPEN) { sock.send(...); }` with no else, no queue, no retry.

It failed **asymmetrically**, which is why it hid: a DOWNLOAD ack fires minutes after page load with
the socket certainly open, while a **HIT** ack fires milliseconds after the grant arrives. So the
devices whose acks vanish are exactly the ones that already hold the segment. sign1screen23 logged
`HIT token=seg_4298b787efa0_0 bytes=66665122` while `/api/discovery/devices` still reported
`propagationPercent 0.0` with that token absent from `cachedSegments`.

The cost is not one stale field. A terminal ack is what calls `PrecacheWindow.advance()`, and
`reconcile_group_cache` returns early while `display_id in precache_windows` — so **one dropped ack
freezes the whole group's cache self-heal** until that grant's deadline expires (up to
`PRECACHE_ACK_MAX` = 1800 s). A completed pull becomes a half-hour stall for every other screen.
Via §1.17 it also leaves that screen routed at the central url, i.e. dark.

**Fix:** `js/mmAckQueue.js` retains `CACHED`/`CACHE_FAILED` and retries on a fixed 3 s timer (not the
GoTime tick, which stops exactly when there is a backlog). Order preserved, so a `CACHED`/
`CACHE_FAILED` pair cannot invert. `CACHE_PROGRESS` is deliberately never retained — replaying it
would refresh a deadline on expired evidence. Bounded at 64, dropping oldest.

**Scope limit:** survives a transport blip, not a page reload — the queue is in memory. The durable
answer is for the client to enumerate its cache dir at startup and report the inventory, so the
server's record self-corrects instead of depending on catching every ack. **Not done.** A device that
loses its cache is still invisible to the server.

## 1.20 The client-pull machinery is unobservable from outside the process — **[FIXED 2026-09-29]**
**`server.py` (`precache_windows`, `_send_precache`, `reconcile_group_cache`) · Medium (operability)** —
`precache_windows` is an in-memory dict with no accessor, `_send_precache` logs at INFO while the
server runs at WARNING, and the reconcile had **five silent early returns**.

On 2026-09-26 two canaries sat with an empty cache and `propagationPercent 0.0` while not one grant
reached them for 14 minutes. Every candidate explanation — gate off, window wedged by a lost ack,
render not READY, nothing missing, grant delivered into a stale SockJS session — fitted the available
evidence *equally well*, and none could be checked. Hours went into arguing unfalsifiable hypotheses.

**Fix:**
- `PrecacheWindow.snapshot(now, timeout_s)` — each grant's own age/deadline/overdue. Per-grant because
  that is where the subtlety is: two grants issued in the same instant can carry very different
  deadlines, and a progress `touch` swaps one for the short stall window. Non-mutating.
- `reconcile_decision(display_id)` returns the go/no-go **and the reason**, and
  `reconcile_group_cache` *obeys it* — so the explanation cannot drift from the behaviour.
- `GET /debug/precache` exposes both, plus each grant's url/segToken/assetBytes.
- A grant reaching its deadline without an ack now logs at **WARNING**.

This paid for itself immediately: the deadline reverting from `90.0` to the size-aware value is a
visible, external signature of a *fresh* grant, which is how §1.21's and §1.18's proofs were confirmed
server-side without touching the device.

## 1.21 A chunk's `Content-Range` is verified only on the FIRST chunk — **[FIXED + PROVEN ON-WALL 2026-10-12]**
**`tweak/mmcache/Tweak.x` (`mm_download`) · High (silent corruption)** — the parse lived inside
`if (total < 0)`, whose job was to learn the entity size. Every later chunk's window went unverified,
so a reply that did not answer the request just made was **appended at the wrong offset, silently**.

On the wall it surfaced as an over-read — `got` exceeding the advertised total — with gross chunk
inflation:

| screen | TRUNCATED | chunks used | chunks needed | after fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sign1screen13 | 23 | **404** | 70 | **70** |
| sign1screen2 | 2 | **309** | 73 | **73** |
| sign1screen20 | 6 | 237 | 62 | **62** |
| sign1screen1 (old build) | 10 | 85 | 85 | — |

Every post-fix pull was md5-identical to source. sign1screen1 still on the older `eae0e7fb` build
shows the same signature, so this predates §1.18's guard and was fleet-wide.

**The over-read was the LUCKY case.** It was caught only because the count overshot and the
completeness check rejected the file. A mis-delivered chunk that made the bytes sum to exactly `total`
would have **passed** that check holding wrong bytes — an unplayable cached segment every later HIT
would serve, with nothing able to tell. Possible contributor to the long-standing verr=3 mystery;
unproven.

**The server is not at fault**, by measurement: 62 sequential and 36 concurrent 1 MB windows against
the live `media_handler` all returned the exact requested length *and* byte-identical content. The
mismatch arises between NSURLConnection and the loop — `sendSynchronousRequest:` consults the shared
NSURLCache on iOS 5 and the server sends no `Cache-Control` on its 206s, so a cached partial served
against a different Range is the leading suspect. The request therefore also sets
`NSURLRequestReloadIgnoringLocalCacheData`.

**Fix:** `mm_chunk_verify(hdr, want_start, want_end, body_len, known_total)` in `mmrange.h` — the
Content-Range must parse, the total must match the one already learned (`ENTITY_CHANGED`), first/last
must equal the window requested (`WRONG_WINDOW`), and the body length must equal that window
(`BAD_LENGTH`). Distinct codes because which check failed separates cache from server from network.
On rejection the partial is **kept** (each byte already written was verified against its own window,
so it stays a valid prefix); `ENTITY_CHANGED` is the exception, where continuing would splice two
videos.

**Length alone is not sufficient, and that is now demonstrated rather than argued.** A fault injected
on 2026-10-12 returned a correctly-sized body for the wrong window:
```
chunk REJECTED reason=wrong-window want=10485760-11534335 got_len=1048576 cr=bytes 10485760-11534336/94889900
```
`got_len` **matched what was requested**. A length-only check would have accepted it and written 1 MB
at the wrong offset. Only the first/last comparison caught it.

**Proof matrix (sign1screen12, 94,889,900 B; fault hook env-gated, one-shot, scoped to one clientKey
by path substring, used once and reverted — never committed):**

| code | reached on-wall | how |
|---|---|---|
| `WRONG_WINDOW` | **yes** (×2) | injected wrong window, correct length |
| `BAD_LENGTH` | **yes** | truthful Content-Range, body one byte short |
| `ENTITY_CHANGED` | intercepted upstream | the ETag check fires first — proven separately by `touch`ing the asset mid-pull (ETag is `mtime_ns-size`), giving `entity changed … discarding partial`, zero content change (md5 identical before/after) |
| `BAD_HEADER` | no | needs a server with no/garbled Content-Range; unit tests only |

Both rejections kept the partial and **resumed from the exact offset** — `resume from=5242880` then
`resume from=10485760` — completing in 81 and 86 chunks (91 − 10, 91 − 5) md5-identical to source.

## 1.22 Twisted's reactor is started by an unlocked check-then-act — a fleet launch loses VNC taps — **[FIXED + PROVEN ON-WALL 2026-10-12]**
**`server.py` (`_get_pooled_vnc`) + `vncdotool/api.py:168` · Medium-High (operability)** — the reactor
is a PROCESS-WIDE singleton that may be started once, but vncdotool 1.3.0 starts it with:

```python
if not reactor.running:
    _THREAD = threading.Thread(target=reactor.run, ...); _THREAD.start()
```

`ReactorBase.startRunning()` guards on its *internal* `_started`, set synchronously, while the public
`running` that vncdotool polls is set later, after the startup event. That gap is the race, and
nothing serialises it. `_get_pooled_vnc` handshakes outside `_veency_lock` **on purpose** — so one
dozing iPad cannot block 23 others — so a fleet-wide `RUN_SCRIPT start` calls `api.connect()` 24 times
at once: several read False, several spawn a reactor thread, and every loser raises inside a thread
where nothing handles it.

**Why it matters more than a logged traceback:** the losing thread dies **with its pending taps**. An
iPad-1 webclip needs one real tap to start video, so those screens never launch — seen as "screen20
did not come back" on 09-29 and 09-30 with **no crash report on the device**, because the process never
started rather than died. A second `RUN_SCRIPT start` fixed it each time, which is what made it look
like generic "VNC tap flakiness". It also clobbers vncdotool's `_THREAD` (so a later `api.shutdown()`
joins the wrong thread) and starts a second `PythonLoggingObserver`.

**Fix:** `mosaicmesh/vnc_reactor.py` starts it once and does not return until `running` is True — after
which vncdotool takes its false branch and never spawns a thread. Only the one-time start is
serialised; the 24 handshakes stay parallel (serialising `api.connect()` would cost ~24 sequential RFB
handshakes per launch). `_started` is the durable guard, not `reactor.running`: Twisted forbids
restarting a stopped reactor, so `running` going False later means *stopped*, never *start again*.

**Proof:** the identical sequence (`stop → login → start`, 24 devices each, ~1 min after boot on a
fresh process) before and after:

| | pre-fix (PID 12820) | post-fix (PID 22892) |
|---|---|---|
| `Exception in thread Twisted Reactor` | **2** | **0** |
| `ReactorAlreadyRunning` lines | **4** | **0** |
| VNC tap failures | 1 | **0** |

The only tap failures afterwards were `veency port unreachable` — the raw-socket pre-check correctly
refusing a dozing iPad, a different mechanism.

# PART 3 — Themes

1. **Silent-success failure modes.** §1.3 reports READY while blocking playback; §1.1 reports online for a dead device; §1.4 reports RENDERING for a failed job. Each hides from the operator, and §1.1 has already cost you a debugging cycle via the lying `online` field. A state-consistency assertion pass would catch all three.
2. **Reconnect storms compound.** §1.1 + §1.2 interact at exactly the worst moment.
3. **Event-loop blocking** (§1.6, §1.12, OpenCV-on-loop) delays `SERVERTIME` — server hygiene and wall sync touch the same loop.
4. **The sync investigation's recurring pattern is sound builds against unmeasured premises** (PI, feedforward, recvTs, cold-load watchdog — each correct in isolation, each not the dominant term). §0.3 is deliberately a measurement, not a build.
5. **One pull, three independent silent-loss mechanisms** (§1.18 concurrent writers, §1.19 dropped acks, §1.21 unverified windows). Each was individually survivable and each hid the others: duplicates made resume unreachable, so no transfer accumulated progress; a dropped ack froze the group's self-heal for up to 30 min, so the retries that *should* have accumulated arrived far apart; and nothing logged any of it (§1.20), so every investigation had to re-derive the state from device-side forensics. That compound is the real explanation for "11 of 24 screens never held a byte across 26 re-issued grants" — no single one of the three accounts for it.
6. **Observability gaps cost more than the defects.** §1.20 and §1.23 were not bugs in behaviour, and both cost more hours than the bugs they concealed. In each case the information was already in the function's hands and simply not printed. The pattern to watch for: a decision with several early returns that logs on none of them.
